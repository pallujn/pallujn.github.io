Brain Training for Dogs: an honest look

Adrienne Farricelli is a professional dog trainer. On her official about page, she describes starting hands-on work with dogs in 2006, becoming certified by the Italian Association for Dog Trainers and Canine Consultants in 2010 after more than 200 hours of apprenticeship, and later earning CPDT-KA certification from the Certification Council for Professional Dog Trainers in the United States. She describes herself as a force-free, positive-reinforcement trainer living in Arizona with her husband and two Rottweilers, Petra and Einstein. CPDT-KA is a real, independently administered credential, not a guarantee that every lesson will work for every dog.

According to the official sales page, the members’ area includes more than 21 games in seven levels (Preschool through Einstein); video demonstrations plus written instructions; troubleshooting sections; seven trick-training videos; Obedience 101 and a section on fading food lures; “Adrienne’s Archive,” described as over 100 in-depth articles, plus a puppy section covering potty training, crate training, socialization, and bite inhibition; a bonus course, Behavior Training for Dogs; a private forum; and a way to submit questions. You can try one free sample, “The Airplane Game,” on the official home page.

On November 20, 2026, the official sales page listed the course and bonuses at $67, presented as a special launch price reduced from $127. That “launch” framing has been used on the page for a long time, so treat $67 as the price we saw, not as a one-hour countdown. The page does not describe a subscription. Confirm the total at checkout. The same page promises a 60-day unconditional money-back guarantee. If you buy, save your receipt. Purchases are processed through ClickBank, which also has its own refund process. Follow the current refund instructions on your receipt.

What we like. The method is force-free and reward-based, not shock, prong, or “dominance” theater. The game structure treats mental work as part of training, which is a good match for bored or busy dogs. The puppy section and the article archive, if they are as broad as the sales page describes, make the $67 price easier to justify than a single-topic video. A 60-day guarantee is more generous than many 30-day course policies.

What we do not like. The sales page is a hard sell. It uses language we would not use ourselves: that the system can “QUICKLY eliminate any behavioral problem,” that almost any problem can be “quickly and easily cured,” and that you should hurry because the program may not always be available. That is advertising, not a training promise we stand behind. The course is also still do-it-yourself. A forum and a question form are helpful; they are not a trainer in your driveway. And we would not choose this as the primary plan for serious aggression, a bite history, or severe separation-related panic. The sales page lists a wide net of problems. A wide net is not the same as the right tool.

A good fit if you want an affordable, force-free program you can use at home, your dog needs a job for the brain or everyday manners, and you will practice in short sessions. A poor fit if you need hands-on help with a safety issue, you want only live coaching, or you expect a download to change your dog from the couch.

SpiritDog Training: when a specialist course is worth more money

SpiritDog is a library of on-demand courses taught by head trainer Steffi Trott. Her official bio says she has been teaching in person and online since 2013, specializes in dog cognition and behavior, and has studied with named European and U.S. trainers. The official materials we read do not list a credential equivalent to CPDT-KA.

The Basic Obedience course was $115 (usually $199), with 43 lessons, lifetime access, a certificate on completion, and a 30-day money-back guarantee. The public curriculum includes attention games, sit and down, stays, loose-leash walking, door manners, and mat work. Just Listen, focused on working under distractions, was $149 (usually $199) for 51 lessons. The homepage also listed bundles at $750 and an Ultimate Reactivity Reset System at $997. The homepage and FAQ state a 30-day money-back guarantee. Believe the checkout policy.

What SpiritDog does well is specificity and instructor questions in the comments. A single course already costs more than Brain Training for Dogs, and the bundles are in a different budget category. A reactivity bundle is not a stand-in for a veterinary behaviorist if your dog is a safety risk.

Zak George: the free course you should not ignore

Zak George has spent years putting humane, reward-based training in front of a very large public audience. His official site is plain about the philosophy: you should not have to choose between being effective and being ethical.

The official 30 Day Perfect Pup course is free. You register with an email and can follow along on the Pupford website or app. Zak’s page says a purchase of Pupford products is not required. The 30 video lessons can be used in order, repeated, or skipped, and are designed for puppies and older dogs. Official topics include leave it and attention, sit, down, stand, crate and potty training, stay, loose-leash walking, recalls, fading food rewards, and problems such as biting, chewing, and jumping.

That is an excellent first move, especially for a new puppy household. What it is not: a paid all-in-one archive, or a specialist class with instructor comments in a paid classroom. Zak’s site also offers other free training-help resources, and optional monthly support starting at $2 to fund a project called Cops + Dogs — not a training course, and not required to use 30 Day Perfect Pup.

So which one is worth it?

There is no single winner, but there is a sensible order: start with Zak George’s free 30 Day Perfect Pup if money is tight; look at Brain Training for Dogs if you want to pay once for a force-free, game-based program and a large archive, with your eyes open about the sales language; pick a SpiritDog course if you have a specific goal and want instructor questions; and get local, qualified help if anyone is unsafe. You can also mix them. Using a free lesson, a checklist habit, and later a paid course is not indecision. It is using tools in the right order.

FAQ

Is Brain Training for Dogs a scam?

We do not see evidence that it is a fake product. It is a real online course from a trainer who lists CPDT-KA certification, sold through a major digital retailer with a 60-day refund window. The marketing promises more than any course can guarantee. Those two things can be true at the same time.

Do I have to keep paying a subscription?

The sales pages we checked described one-time purchases for Brain Training for Dogs and for SpiritDog’s Basic Obedience and Just Listen courses, and no course fee for 30 Day Perfect Pup. Always read the checkout screen.

Can an online course fix reactivity or aggression?

It can help some owners practice foundation skills. It is not an appropriate sole plan for a bite risk or a dog who is injuring themselves. Get qualified in-person help.

What if I buy Brain Training for Dogs and it is not for us?

Use the 60-day guarantee as written on the official site and on your receipt after a fair try in short sessions.

Should I talk to my vet first?

Yes, if the behavior is new, if you suspect pain, or if anything might be medical.
